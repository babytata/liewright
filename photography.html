Photography & Fine Art

Define the Era — Capturing the Space

By Liewright Studio July 20, 2026 3 min read
Figure 1: Overhead art installation in Palmer Alley, showcasing vibrant, translucent colors catching natural light.

Exploring the tension between form, light, and public spaces, this collection translates the living, shifting expressions of design into visual archives.

In 2026, design is defined by constant movement—borrowed, remixed, and forward-looking. To document this evolution, these photographic studies capture instances where architecture and art interact with public routines. The objects, the light, and the physical spaces become coordinates of a larger visual narrative.

Every public space has a silent rhythm. As people move through alleys, plazas, and city centers, they encounter unexpected configurations of color and scale overhead. The selected installation captures this open-air gallery quality, redefining how the space feels in motion.

Figure 2: Minimalist study of geometric shadows and warm afternoon sunlight cast across concrete walls.

Shadows are not empty spaces; they are structured forms. By capturing the sharp geometric lines formed by the late afternoon sun on concrete blocks, these frames analyze how light reshapes architectural textures. The result is a stark, high-contrast study in silence and permanence.

Figure 3: Moody atmospheric fog and silhouettes along the rugged, silent coastlines.

Contrast this geometric rigidity with the soft, unpredictable lines of natural environments. The misty coastal cliffs captured here isolate single silhouettes against vast atmospheric gradients. It is an exploration of scale, mood, and quiet contemplation away from the city structure.
